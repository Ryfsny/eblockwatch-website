What I have learnt

Crime depends on surprise. Remove the surprise and you remove the advantage.

Surprise is their weapon

An attack at a robot is over in seconds. Whatever happens after that is cleanup. Awareness before the event is worth more than any button pressed after it.

Pressing a panic button after an incident is like moaning at the ref after the game.

The cameras watch. Nobody connects them.

We have spent billions on cameras, patrols and apps, and people are still robbed at the same robots. The problem is not a shortage of equipment. It is a shortage of connection.

Intelligence comes from people

Every report is a piece of a larger picture. Nobody is obliged to send one. But when someone does, it goes to one place, where it can be put together and acted on.

Identity is defence

People have always been safer together than alone. A visible eblockwatch sticker says you are not alone, and that matters more than it seems.

Touch one, you touch all of us.

Loyalty cannot be bought

Trust cannot be bought, and it cannot be rushed. Mine was built over 25 years with community groups and local security, one relationship at a time. That trust is what answers when I call.

We look after you between safe zones

Home, work, school. Those are your safe zones. The risk lives on the road between them. That is where we watch, and nothing escalates while you are home.

One general decides

You are not expected to act. Your strength is your connection: your community group and the local security who know you. Decisions are made in one place, by someone who sees the whole picture. Too many voices in a crisis produce chaos.

I'm not saving the world. I'm saving my members.
